Making Tax Digital for sole traders earning over £30,000

If you’re self-employed and your income was over £30,000 in the 2025–26 tax year, you must use Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax from 6 April 2027. Here’s what that means in plain English.

Who has to use it, and when

It depends on your qualifying income: everything you earn from self-employment and property, added together, before expenses. Wages from a job, pensions and savings interest don’t count.

Example: a plumber takes £38,000 from jobs in 2025–26 and spends £12,000 on van, tools and materials. Their profit is £26,000, but their qualifying income is £38,000, so they start MTD in April 2027.

What you have to do

  • Keep digital records. Record each sale and cost in software, not on paper.
  • Use HMRC-recognised software. It sends your figures to HMRC for you. Some options are free.
  • Send a quarterly update. A short summary of income and expenses, four times a year. It isn’t a tax return and you don’t pay tax with it.
  • Do your tax return through the software by 31 January after the tax year ends, as now.

Late updates and returns can build up penalty points under HMRC’s points-based system. Check GOV.UK for how penalties apply in your first year.

What to do now

  1. Work out your 2025–26 qualifying income (use the free checker).
  2. Make sure you’re registered for Self Assessment. You need this before you can sign up.
  3. Pick MTD software. See our software guide.
  4. Start recording income and expenses in it before April, so the first quarter isn’t a scramble.
  5. Sign up for MTD on GOV.UK, or ask an accountant to sign you up as your agent.

Can I be exempt?

Some people can apply for an exemption, for example if age, disability, where you live or religious beliefs mean you can’t reasonably use digital tools. Search GOV.UK for “Making Tax Digital exemption” to check.

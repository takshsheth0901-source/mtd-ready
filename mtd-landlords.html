Making Tax Digital for landlords

If you own property personally (not through a company) and your rental income plus any self-employment income was over £30,000 in 2025–26, you must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2027.

How your rent is counted

  • Gross rent, before costs. Use the rent you’re owed, before mortgage interest, repairs, insurance or agent fees.
  • Letting agent fees don’t reduce it. If your agent collects £1,500 a month and keeps £150, your rent is still £1,500.
  • Add any self-employment income. A landlord with £22,000 of rent who also earns £12,000 freelancing has £34,000 of qualifying income.
  • Jointly owned property: you count your own share of the rent. Two owners splitting £40,000 equally have £20,000 each.

What changes for landlords

  • Record rent and property costs digitally, in HMRC-recognised software.
  • Send a short quarterly summary by 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.
  • Do your tax return through the software by 31 January, as now.

If you have several properties, good software lets you tag each income and cost to a property, so you can see which ones actually make money.

Tips that save time

  • Use a separate bank account for your rentals and connect it to your software, so most transactions come in automatically.
  • Ask your letting agent for monthly statements in a digital format you can import.
  • Photograph receipts for repairs and certificates as you go, rather than saving them for January.

What to do now

  1. Check your 2025–26 income with the free checker.
  2. Make sure you’re registered for Self Assessment.
  3. Choose software that handles property income. See the software guide.
  4. Sign up on GOV.UK, or have an accountant do it for you.
